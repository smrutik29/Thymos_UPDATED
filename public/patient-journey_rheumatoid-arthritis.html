How rheumatoid arthritis is typically treated

What changes long-term
🔁 Surgery doesn't stop rheumatoid arthritis progression. Joint replacement addresses mechanical damage but does not modify the underlying disease. Untreated rheumatoid arthritis will continue to attack other joints — cervical spine, shoulders, wrists, and small joints of the hands are all at risk. Ongoing DMARD therapy is typically maintained post-operatively.
🦠 Elevated infection risk. rheumatoid arthritis patients on immunosuppressive therapy have a 1.5–2.5× higher rate of periprosthetic joint infection than osteoarthritis patients. Biologic DMARDs and JAK inhibitors are typically held 1–5 half-lives before elective surgery and restarted after wound healing — a window requiring close coordination with your rheumatologist.12
🧠 Mental health comorbidity is under-addressed. Major depressive disorder affects ~16.8% of rheumatoid arthritis patients — 2–3× the general population rate of 6.6%.13 Anxiety disorders affect an additional 13.5% on top of that. Depression independently predicts worse pain scores, reduced treatment adherence, and higher healthcare costs — and is rarely screened for at rheumatology visits.
❤️ Cardiovascular risk doubles. rheumatoid arthritis carries a cardiovascular risk equivalent to type 2 diabetes — including 2× the rate of myocardial infarction and a 70% increased risk of stroke vs. age-matched controls.14 This is driven by the cumulative inflammatory burden and is rarely discussed proactively in a rheumatology visit.
🦴 Lymphoma risk up to 26×. rheumatoid arthritis is associated with a markedly elevated risk of haematological malignancies, particularly lymphoma — driven by cumulative inflammatory load.14 Patients with uncontrolled disease for longer periods carry disproportionately higher risk.
⚠ Perioperative DMARD management is complex
Guidelines from ACR/AAHKS recommend holding biologics and JAK inhibitors before elective arthroplasty. The timing varies by drug half-life. This coordination requires explicit planning with your rheumatologist — it is not automatic and not always initiated. Bring it up early if you are considering surgery.

The Other Side of the Bill

What your insurer is paying

Rheumatoid arthritis imposes a substantial and rising cost on commercial insurers. Biologic and targeted synthetic drugs — not the joint replacement surgery patients often fear most — are the dominant driver, and step therapy requirements delay rather than reduce the cumulative spend.

Biologic-treated rheumatoid arthritis patients cost payers substantially more than those on conventional DMARDs alone — commercial claims analyses report total annual healthcare costs 3–4× higher once a patient escalates to biologic or targeted therapy.18 Roughly 1 in 5 rheumatoid arthritis patients will eventually need a knee or hip replacement, adding a further one-time cost spike on top of ongoing drug spend.10
⚑ Why early, adequate treatment saves payers money
Step therapy requirements mandate a methotrexate trial — and often a first anti-TNF failure — before newer mechanisms are approved, adding months of active, undertreated inflammation. That inflammation accrues irreversible joint damage that drives the ~20% lifetime risk of knee or hip replacement,10 the elevated cardiovascular risk,14 and the work disability3 that make up the majority of the downstream cost burden of rheumatoid arthritis — costs that land on the payer regardless of which drug tier delayed adequate control.
