How psoriatic arthritis is typically treated

Most rheumatologists and insurance plans require NSAID and Methotrexate failure before approving a biologic. Each step can take 3–6 months to fail, while joint erosion accumulates in ways that later treatment cannot undo.

What happens while the diagnosis is pending
🔪 Surgery becomes a substitute for disease control. In a real-world cohort, the annual rate of musculoskeletal surgery was significantly higher during the diagnostic-delay period than after psoriatic arthritis was formally diagnosed and treated.9
📈 The claims history starts rising years before anyone names the disease. Healthcare costs begin increasing approximately 5 years before an eventual psoriatic arthritis diagnosis — driven substantially by MSK surgeries and unexplained joint complaints.10
🦴 Delay of even 6–12 months predicts worse function. Multiple studies link diagnostic delays of this length to structural joint damage and poorer long-term functional outcomes that later biologic therapy cannot reverse.2
🎯 Awareness gaps, not biology, drive much of the delay. Clinical awareness of psoriatic arthritis compared to rheumatoid arthritis remains poor in general community practice, contributing to slow rheumatology referral.2
⚠ The biologic ladder starts the clock too late
Step therapy sequences which conventional DMARD to try first, then which biologic — but none of that addresses the years patients often spend with unrecognized joint symptoms before any therapy starts. By the time Step 1 begins, damage may already be underway.

⚑ Where the real savings are being missed
Step therapy controls which biologic is tried first — but the biggest avoidable cost sits upstream of it, in the ~5 years of rising claims and elevated surgery rates before psoriatic arthritis is even diagnosed. Faster rheumatology referral, not a different biologic sequence, is where the larger savings opportunity lives.
