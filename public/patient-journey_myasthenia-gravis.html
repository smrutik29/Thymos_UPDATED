What durability actually looks like
🔁 Response isn't a one-time event — it's a recurring bet. Efgartigimod is given in repeated 4-week cycles; in real-world cohorts, over a third of patients ultimately discontinued due to insufficient or non-durable efficacy despite initial response.7
💸 Independent cost watchdogs have called the pricing indefensible. ICER found eculizumab's ~$653,100/year list price would need a 97–98% discount to meet standard cost-effectiveness thresholds — among the largest pricing gaps flagged for any FDA-approved drug.3
🫁 Crisis remains the disease's worst-case outcome even on modern therapy. Myasthenic crisis with respiratory failure can still occur despite treatment, requiring ICU admission, mechanical ventilation, and rescue therapy (IVIG or plasma exchange) layered on top of maintenance biologic costs.4
🧬 Antibody status changes what actually works. Eculizumab's evidence base is strongest in AChR-antibody-positive patients; efficacy in MuSK-positive or seronegative disease is far less established, meaning a meaningful subset of patients don't have a clearly proven biologic option.1
⚠ The ladder gets exponentially more expensive right when disease control matters most
Pyridostigmine costs hundreds of dollars a year. The complement inhibitors reserved for refractory, crisis-prone disease cost 500–1,000× more — a cost curve that tracks disease severity almost perfectly, with very little room in between.
