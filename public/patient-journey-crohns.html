What changes long-term
♻️ Surgery doesn't cure Crohn's. Unlike UC, Crohn's involves the full GI tract. Endoscopic recurrence at the anastomosis site occurs in 65–90% of patients within 12 months post-resection without maintenance therapy.12
🩺 Ostomy risk: 15–30% of Crohn's patients require a temporary or permanent ostomy. Permanent ostomy is more likely in perianal or colonic disease — significantly affecting body image, sexual function, and daily routines.
🧠 Mental health impact. Depression affects 34–39% of Crohn's patients post-surgery — with women carrying roughly 1.8× the odds of clinical depression compared to men (aOR 1.77).14 Patients diagnosed before age 35 have substantially higher rates of post-surgical depression. Both sexes are chronically underscreened.
💊 Lifelong nutritional management. Ileocecal resection — the most common procedure — removes the site of B12 absorption. Deficiencies in B12, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K), iron, zinc, and magnesium often require permanent supplementation or monitoring.
🔁 High reoperation burden. ~50% of Crohn's patients who have a bowel resection require at least one additional surgery within 10 years. Each subsequent resection carries a greater risk of short bowel syndrome.
⚠ Discuss prevention of recurrence
Guidelines recommend post-operative colonoscopy at 6–12 months and early initiation of biologic maintenance therapy to reduce endoscopic recurrence. Discuss this proactively with your GI — it is not always offered without prompting.

💊
~50% of Crohn's patients on adalimumab lose clinical response within 2 years — requiring dose escalation, a switch, or surgery8

~70% of Crohn's patients undergo at least one bowel surgery within 10 years of diagnosis — making Crohn's one of the highest-surgery chronic diseases11
