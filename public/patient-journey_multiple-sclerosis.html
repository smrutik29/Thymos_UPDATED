How multiple sclerosis is typically treated

Most doctors and insurance plans follow step therapy — starting with lower-efficacy injectables before approving high-efficacy biologics. The stakes are high: multiple sclerosis lesions can accumulate silently between relapses, and each year without adequate disease control carries a compounding disability burden.

What changes as disability accumulates
🧠 Cognitive impairment affects ~50% of patients. Processing speed, working memory, and verbal fluency are the most commonly affected domains. Cognitive disability is often more functionally disruptive than physical disability — yet is underscreened and rarely addressed at neurology visits.13
😞 Depression is the most common psychiatric comorbidity. Major depressive disorder affects approximately 50% of people with multiple sclerosis over their lifetime — nearly 3× the general population rate — and is an independent predictor of quality of life, treatment adherence, and survival.14
💼 Employment loss is nearly universal at advanced stages. ~40% of multiple sclerosis patients leave employment within 5 years of SPMS diagnosis. Indirect productivity costs from absenteeism and early exit from the workforce can exceed the direct drug costs of DMT therapy — yet are never put on the same receipt.10
🧬 ~72% of all disability-worsening events are PIRA — not caused by relapses. In the Italian Multiple Sclerosis Registry cohort of 16,130 relapsing-onset patients, PIRA accounted for 72.3% of all confirmed disability accrual events over a mean follow-up of 11.8 years.16 A 48-study systematic review in JAMA Neurology (2023) found PIRA occurs in roughly 5% of RRMS patients per year and causes at least 50% of all disability accrual events in typical RRMS — even in patients who appear relapse-free on treatment. This means inflammation suppression alone cannot protect against the neurodegeneration driving long-term disability.
❤️ Life expectancy is reduced by 7–10 years. multiple sclerosis patients die earlier primarily from secondary complications: aspiration pneumonia, urinary tract infections, immobility-related conditions, and cardiovascular disease.12 Suicide accounts for 7.5× the general population rate in the multiple sclerosis population.
⚠ Progression is not inevitable — timing matters most
The strongest modifiable predictor of SPMS conversion is the degree of inflammation control in the first 5 years of disease. Studies show that patients treated early with high-efficacy therapies have significantly lower conversion rates. The current step-therapy system can delay access to these therapies by years.

⚑ What step therapy costs your plan in the long run
Early initiation of high-efficacy DMT is associated with $4,296 lower multiple sclerosis-related costs per year vs. delayed treatment — driven by fewer relapses, hospitalizations, and disability-related services.21 Step therapy requirements that delay access to high-efficacy therapies generate near-term drug cost savings that are offset by downstream relapse, hospitalisation, and long-term disability management costs — a trade-off that is rarely made transparent to members.
